## 1. The spine, in one sentence

> I rebuilt a published USGS method as open code, tried five times to break it,
> and this is the measured boundary between what free satellite imagery can and
> cannot tell you about mine drainage — which is why my fieldwork is designed
> the way it is.

**The thesis sentence for the closing slide** (already written, from
`validation/ACCURACY_ASSESSMENT.md` §7): a single continuous band ratio
separates AMD-affected water from chemically verified clean water at
**monitored locations** in open acid-drainage terrain, and **ranks** severity
within a mineral district; it does not predict concentration across districts,
does not find unknown sites, and does not transfer to forested neutral-pH coal
drainage.

## 4. The Q&A sheet

A separate one-page PDF, twelve questions, each with the number and the honest
answer. The hardest six, with the answers already in the record:

1. **"Do you have your own field data?"** — No. Every number is against
   agency-measured chemistry (USGS NWIS + EPA via the Water Quality Portal):
   n=86 source points, n=75 paired. The field campaign is **pre-registered as of
   19 September**, verdict rules fixed before the first sample.
2. **"Why is Leadville flat?"** — Unresolved. Candidates: dynamic range,
   Superfund remediation history, mineralogy. It is reported precisely because it
   did not work.
3. **"Isn't agreement with the USGS map validation?"** — No: replica fidelity.
   κ = 0.55, and the 88% is carried by vegetation at an AMD base rate of ~0.5%.
4. **"Can you see sulfate?"** — No, and nobody can: sulfate has no VNIR
   absorption. Any apparent signal is iron, turbidity or vegetation that
   co-varies with it.
5. **"Your blind search had low power."** — Correct, and stated in advance:
   0.54 at a threefold lift, 0.80 at fourfold. A large lift is excluded; a
   modest one is not.
6. **"Where are the isotopes and the magnetism from your abstract?"** — Not
   collected. VSM access is arranged with Dr. Wagner at KSU (blocked on having
   proper samples); the WSU δ³⁴S quote is $1,306 for 60 samples; both are
   registered in the field plan, magnetism as estimation-only.

**Deviations from the plan, and why:**

- **Figures carry no title or caption** (`SLIDE_MODE`, the default). The first
  build duplicated the slide title inside the image and wasted a third of the
  projector. `--with-titles` restores the standalone version for a paper.
- **Figure D is one row of four, tall**, not 2\u00d72. The free area on a 16:9 slide
  is about 12.5 \u00d7 5 inches, so an aspect near 2.4 fills it; a square block of
  panels renders smaller.
- **Figure H splits in-lake from inflow-stream stations.** The first version
  pooled them, which mixed two populations silently \u2014 and the inflow gradient
  (12\u20131,370 mg/L) is the better story, since that is what the campaign samples.
- **No GEE screenshot on slide 4.** It would need a fresh Earth Engine session;
  the slide works as text. Add one if you want it.

**Traceability built in:** `make_talk_figures.py` re-reads each source report at
run time and aborts if a literal no longer appears in it, and figure D recomputes
\u03c1 from the raw extractions and aborts unless it reproduces +0.568 at n = 75 with
the four per-district values. A figure cannot silently drift from the record.

**Not done, and deliberately:** a geologic basemap under the study-area map (an
ArcGIS job, optional), and a QR code on slide 13 (add before travelling).
